# Mission: AI and Transformers

## Why

To build a genuine, ground-up understanding of how modern AI systems work — not the
surface tour, but the real mechanics, history, and trade-offs. The goal is to reach
the point where I can read primary AI research, reason confidently about what today's
models can and cannot do, and form my own views about where the field is heading —
grounded in *why* each idea appeared, not just that it did.

## Success looks like

- Reading a paper like "Attention Is All You Need" and understanding every design
  decision at the mechanical level, not just the headline idea
- Explaining the entire conceptual arc — from McCulloch-Pitts through transformers
  to modern foundation models — in my own words, with the math
- Forming and pressure-testing my own predictions about where AI goes next, with
  enough historical grounding to spot when a "new" idea is really an old one
- Identifying when a model's failure is architectural vs. a training/data problem

## Constraints

- Can write code; math (linear algebra, calculus, probability) needs rebuilding
  alongside each concept — not skipped or dumbed down
- Learning via skills: `/teach` for structured lessons, `/grill-me` for recall
  practice, `/ask-matt` to pressure-test conclusions
- No deadline; depth over speed

## Out of scope

- Practical "how to call the API" usage — that's engineering, not understanding
- Survey-level coverage of every model that ever existed
- ML operations, deployment, or infrastructure concerns
